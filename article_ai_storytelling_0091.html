The Narrative Wall: Why AI Characters Change Mid-Scene

As identified for those attempting to create narrative clips, hitting the 'Narrative Wall' is common. It’s that moment when your protagonist—perfectly defined in shot one—suddenly has different hair color or clothing in shot two. This failure is rarely due to the model's technical limit, but to a fundamental reliance on a 'Slot Machine' workflow.

The Failure of Text-to-Video

The biggest mistake in AI storytelling is typing a long prompt and hitting generate. This is Text-to-Video, and it gives you zero control over the composition. When the AI has to invent the subject, action, and environment simultaneously, it often loses the thread of character identity mid-shot. In a narrative sequence, this randomness is fatal.

The Sovereignty of the Start Frame

To fix narrative drift, you must anchor your video in a Start Frame. A start frame provides the visual DNA for your entire sequence. By providing an image, you take the 'invention' load off the AI and define the bone structure and lighting environment before animation begins. However, using a bad quality image with 'plasticky AI skin' will only amplify artificiality through every frame.

Rule #1: Garbage In, Garbage Out

Your start frame is responsible for 80% of your final result. For production-ready assets, you must use high-end models like Soul 2, optimized for realistic textures and lighting.
