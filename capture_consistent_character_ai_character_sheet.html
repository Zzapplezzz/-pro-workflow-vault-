The "Text-to-Video" Trap: Why Prompts Fail

Most AI creators treat tools like Higgsfield or Runway like a slot machine. They type a long, complex prompt, hit generate, and hope for a miracle. This is what we call Text-to-Video gambling. The problem is that you have zero control over the composition and the identity of your subject [1]. Without a visual anchor, the AI has to invent the character, the action, and the environment simultaneously, leading to Face Drifting and incoherent physics [1, 2].

If you've noticed your character's eye color or hair style changing between clips, you're experiencing Latent Space Mapping variance [2, 3]. Your brain clocks these subtle errors instantly, making the video feel "cheap" or "uncanny" [1]. To fix this, you must move from being a prompt engineer to a digital cinematographer.

The Secret 80/20 Rule: The Power of the Start Frame

In professional AI filmmaking, the Start Frame (the initial image used for animation) is responsible for 80% of your result [4, 5]. This gives the AI a "Gold Standard" to follow, ensuring that the character remains 100% consistent throughout the animation [2, 4].
