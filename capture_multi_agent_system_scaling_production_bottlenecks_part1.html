Part 1: The Hidden Trap – Why Simple AI Workflows Fail at Scale

The Mathematics of Collapse

Deploying a single AI agent is a feat of engineering; scaling a multi-agent system is a feat of architecture. The "Hidden Trap" lies in the compounding nature of error rates [4]. If an agent executes a ten-step task with a stellar 95% accuracy per step, its overall success rate plummets to just under 60% [5]. In production, this is lethal for your operations.

Most systems die in the "Infinite Polite Loop" [6]. When one flawlessly functioning agent is programmed to verify another, their programmed helpfulness can trigger a recursive loop—endlessly correcting each other until they bankrupt your cloud account just to say "thank you" [6, 7].

From Context Exhaustion to Token Bloat

The second trap is Context Exhaustion and Memory Bloat [6]. Passing raw conversation histories between agents triggers an exponential growth in token counts [6]. Your orchestrator becomes crowded, triggering context window congestion, and the system eventually hallucinations because it confuses the rules of different sub-tasks [8].

To survive at an enterprise level, you need to replace the "lone genius" model with a Digital Assembly Line [9]. This means giving agents narrower instructions and implementing deterministic guardrails to prevent stochastic failures [9].
